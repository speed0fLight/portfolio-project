The research interests of the mars rover project lie in reverse engineering and simulation; the design was done from checking pictures of previous NASA rovers and then reconstructing a CAD Model from the side profile of the Curiosity rover. During the simulation phase, I observed how one side of the rubber wheels motor mounting points distributed a load better than the other side of said wheel (square vs circular bracket) where the circular wheel bracket performed better overall by distributing the load. The simulation used a downforce on the wheel of 10 Newtons on its axle, simulating the chassis weight on the wheel. The dependent variable was the terrain, which was a flat surface consisting of small ridges and bumps to test the springiness of the wheel. The rubber spokes on the wheel are meant to handle bumps in the place of spring shock absorbers, just like the titanium spokes that the Perseverance rover uses. The impact of this project was curating student interest in engineering and showing applications of the project towards NASA’s goals. The project was presented at the S-STEM Scholars & PI Conference in Washington D.C. and was very well received by both students and faculty, leading to other students to explore their state’s local space consortium.
Overall, the material science involved in additively manufacturing a proper wheel was done through simulations with rubber materials.
